## Q1
type: MCQ
marks: 1
subject: general-aptitude
answer: B

Choose the grammatically correct pair of verbs to complete the sentence:

"The chief security officer, along with two network forensics specialists, ______ reviewing the audit logs when the ransomware payload ______ detected on the backup server."

(A) were, was
(B) was, was
(C) was, were
(D) were, were

### Solution
Let us analyze the grammatical rules governing both blanks:

1. **First Blank (Subject-Verb Agreement with Intervening Prepositional Phrases):**
   - The primary subject of the clause is *"The chief security officer"*, which is a singular count noun.
   - The phrase *"along with two network forensics specialists"* is an intervening prepositional modifier. In English grammar, phrases introduced by *"along with"*, *"as well as"*, *"accompanied by"*, or *"in addition to"* do not form a compound subject and do not alter the grammatical number of the subject.
   - Therefore, the verb must agree solely with the singular subject *"The chief security officer"*.
   - The required verb is **was**. (Choosing *"were"* is a common GATE distractor trap that mistakes the plural noun inside the prepositional phrase for the subject).

2. **Second Blank (Subject-Verb Agreement in the Dependent Time Clause):**
   - The subject of the clause *"when the ransomware payload ... detected"* is *"the ransomware payload"*, which is singular.
   - Therefore, the singular verb **was** is required.

Combining both blanks gives: **was, was**.

Evaluating the options:
- (A) Incorrectly uses the plural *"were"* in the first blank.
- (B) **was, was** is correct.
- (C) Incorrectly uses the plural *"were"* in the second blank.
- (D) Incorrectly uses the plural *"were"* in both blanks.

Hence, (B) is the correct option.

Choose the correct pair of words that best fits the blanks in the sentence below:

"The new caching module was engineered to ______ the underlying storage subsystem, while the server rack remained completely ______ during the simulated seismic test."

(A) complement, stationary
(B) compliment, stationary
(C) complement, stationery
(D) compliment, stationery

### Solution
Let us distinguish the homophones and confusable pairs in context:

1. **First Blank (complement vs. compliment):**
   - **Complement** (spelled with an **e**): A verb/noun meaning to add to in a way that enhances, completes, or brings to perfection (*"Her technical skills complement the team"*). In technical architecture, a caching layer *complements* the storage subsystem by completing and enhancing its capabilities.
   - **Compliment** (spelled with an **i**): A noun/verb expressing praise, admiration, or congratulation (*"He received a compliment from his supervisor"*).
   - Therefore, **complement** is required.

2. **Second Blank (stationary vs. stationery):**
   - **Stationary** (spelled with an **a**): An adjective meaning not moving, motionless, fixed in place, or unchanging. A server rack during a seismic vibration test remaining motionless is described as *stationary*.
   - **Stationery** (spelled with an **e**): A noun referring to writing materials, office supplies, envelopes, and paper. (Memory aid: station**e**ry has an **e** for **e**nvelopes and p**e**ns).
   - Therefore, **stationary** is required.

Combining both words yields: **complement, stationary**.

Evaluating the options:
- (A) **complement, stationary** is correct.
- (B) Uses *compliment* (praise) instead of *complement*.
- (C) Uses *stationery* (office supplies) instead of *stationary*.
- (D) Incorrectly uses both confusable counterparts.

Hence, (A) is the correct option.

Two automated sorting machines, $M_1$ and $M_2$, process packages in a logistics hub. Machine $M_1$ is $1.5\text{ times}$ as efficient as machine $M_2$.

Working alone at its constant speed, machine $M_2$ takes $30\text{ hours}$ to process a standard batch of packages.

If both machines work together simultaneously at their respective constant speeds, the total time required to process the same batch of packages is ______ hours.

### Solution
Let us solve using the LCM chairs / unit rate model:

1. **Define Total Work:**
   - Machine $M_2$ completes the batch in $30\text{ hours}$.
   - Let Total Work = $30\text{ units}$.

2. **Determine Efficiency (Rates):**
   - Efficiency is defined as work done per unit time:
     $$R_2 = \frac{30\text{ units}}{30\text{ hours}} = 1\text{ unit/hour}$$
   - Machine $M_1$ is $1.5\text{ times}$ as efficient as $M_2$:
     $$R_1 = 1.5 \times R_2 = 1.5 \times 1 = 1.5\text{ units/hour}$$

3. **Combined Processing Rate:**
   When both machines operate simultaneously:
   $$R_{\text{combined}} = R_1 + R_2 = 1.5 + 1 = 2.5\text{ units/hour}$$

4. **Time Taken Together:**
   $$T = \frac{\text{Total Work}}{R_{\text{combined}}} = \frac{30\text{ units}}{2.5\text{ units/hour}} = \frac{30}{\frac{5}{2}} = \frac{60}{5} = 12\text{ hours}$$

*(Alternative check via individual times: Since $M_1$ is $1.5\times$ as fast, it takes $\frac{30}{1.5} = 20\text{ hours}$ alone. Combined time = $\frac{20 \times 30}{20 + 30} = \frac{600}{50} = 12\text{ hours}$).*

Hence, the answer is 12.

## Q4
type: MCQ

Choose the pair of words that best fills the blanks to make the sentence grammatically meaningful and logically consistent:

"Although the preliminary research proposal suffered from a ______ of empirical benchmark data, the presentation delivered by the lead scientist was remarkably ______, leaving no ambiguity regarding the core methodology."

(A) paucity, lucid
(B) plethora, loquacious
(C) paucity, convoluted
(D) plethora, lucid

### Solution
Let us analyze the logical structure and vocabulary roots:

1. **Signpost Analysis ("Although"):**
   - The conjunction *"Although"* establishes a contrast between the limitation of the proposal and the positive clarity of the presentation.
   - The first clause describes a defect or limitation (*"suffered from a [blank 1] of data"*), requiring a word signifying scarcity or deficiency.
   - The second clause describes the presentation as leaving *"no ambiguity regarding the core methodology"*, requiring a word signifying transparency, clarity, and precision.

2. **Vocabulary & Root Analysis:**
   - **Paucity** (from root `pauci` = few, little): noun meaning the presence of something in only small or insufficient quantities; scarcity or dearth.
   - **Lucid** (from root `luc` / `lum` = light, clear): adjective meaning expressed clearly, luminous, easy to understand.
   - **Plethora**: an excessive amount, overabundance. One does not typically "suffer from a plethora of data" when proposing a research problem unless overwhelmed, but here the contrast word "Although" and "leaving no ambiguity" requires a scarcity vs. clarity contrast.
   - **Loquacious** (from root `loq` = talk, speak): adjective meaning talkative or wordy; does not mean clear or free of ambiguity.
   - **Convoluted**: extremely complex and difficult to follow; contradicts *"leaving no ambiguity"*.

3. **Evaluating the Options:**
   - (A) **paucity, lucid** correctly creates the contrast: despite a scarcity of data, the presentation was clear and unambiguous.
   - (B) *plethora, loquacious* does not fit the contrast and misapplies *loquacious*.
   - (C) *paucity, convoluted* contradicts *"leaving no ambiguity"*.
   - (D) *plethora, lucid* fails to establish the contrast indicated by *"suffered from"*.

Hence, (A) is the correct option.

answer: 30

A large water storage tank is fitted with two pipes: an inlet pipe $P$ and an outlet drain pipe $Q$.

- Inlet pipe $P$ alone can fill the completely empty tank in $12\text{ hours}$.
- When both inlet pipe $P$ and drain pipe $Q$ are opened simultaneously, the completely empty tank takes $20\text{ hours}$ to be filled to full capacity.

If the tank is initially full to capacity and inlet pipe $P$ is turned off, the total time required for drain pipe $Q$ alone to empty the entire tank is ______ hours.

### Solution
Let us apply the LCM unit rate model with positive and negative rates:

1. **Determine Tank Capacity (Total Work):**
   $$\text{Capacity} = \text{LCM}(12, 20) = 60\text{ units}$$

2. **Compute Rates:**
   - Rate of Inlet Pipe $P$ (filling $\implies$ positive):
     $$R_P = \frac{+60\text{ units}}{12\text{ hours}} = +5\text{ units/hour}$$
   - Net Rate when both $P$ and $Q$ operate together:
     $$R_{\text{net}} = \frac{+60\text{ units}}{20\text{ hours}} = +3\text{ units/hour}$$

3. **Determine Drain Rate of Pipe $Q$:**
   Since drain pipe $Q$ removes water:
   $$R_{\text{net}} = R_P - R_Q$$
   $$+3 = 5 - R_Q \implies R_Q = 5 - 3 = 2\text{ units/hour}$$

4. **Time for Drain Pipe $Q$ to Empty the Full Tank:**
   $$T_Q = \frac{\text{Total Capacity}}{R_Q} = \frac{60\text{ units}}{2\text{ units/hour}} = 30\text{ hours}$$

*(Common GATE Trap: Adding the rates instead of subtracting the drain rate, which yields $5 + 3 = 8\text{ units/hr} \implies 7.5\text{ hours}$, confusing an inlet with a drain).*

Hence, the answer is 30.

Read the passage below and answer the question that follows:

"Modern distributed data stores rely heavily on optimistic replication protocols to achieve high write availability and low response latencies across geographically dispersed data centers. Under this paradigm, write operations are acknowledged immediately at local nodes without waiting for synchronous cross-datacenter quorum agreement. Replica convergence is subsequently achieved via asynchronous reconciliation mechanisms such as vector clocks or conflict-free replicated data types (CRDTs). While this design guarantees eventual consistency, it inherently abandons strong isolation guarantees. Consequently, applications executing concurrent transactions on conflicting data records may observe transient anomalies, including stale reads and phantom updates. Software architects frequently attempt to mitigate these anomalies by embedding heuristic conflict-resolution policies into the application business logic. However, data consistency auditors emphasize that heuristic patching at the application layer cannot substitute for protocol-level serializability: as concurrency scales under peak network traffic, subtle interleavings of non-deterministic conflict resolutions inevitably produce irrecoverable business state divergence."

Which one of the following statements is the most valid INFERENCE from the passage?

(A) Optimistic replication protocols guarantee strong isolation across concurrent transactions by leveraging vector clocks.
(B) Relying on heuristic conflict resolution in application code is sufficient to prevent business state divergence under heavy transaction concurrency.
(C) Achieving high write availability through asynchronous reconciliation implies that applications must tolerate the possibility of transient data inconsistencies.
(D) Protocol-level serializability can be achieved in distributed data stores without sacrificing low write latency or immediate local acknowledgments.

### Solution
Let us evaluate each statement strictly against the assertions made in the passage:

- **Evaluating (C):**
  - The passage states that optimistic replication achieves high availability and low latency by acknowledging writes locally and relying on asynchronous reconciliation.
  - It explicitly states: *"While this design guarantees eventual consistency, it inherently abandons strong isolation guarantees. Consequently, applications executing concurrent transactions on conflicting data records may observe transient anomalies, including stale reads and phantom updates."*
  - This directly implies that opting for high write availability via asynchronous reconciliation requires applications to tolerate the potential presence of transient anomalies and inconsistencies.
  - Thus, (C) is a strictly valid inference.

- **Evaluating (A):**
  - The passage explicitly states that optimistic replication *"inherently abandons strong isolation guarantees."* Vector clocks are used for replica convergence, not to guarantee strong isolation. Therefore, (A) directly contradicts the text.

- **Evaluating (B):**
  - The passage explicitly refutes this: *"heuristic patching at the application layer cannot substitute for protocol-level serializability: as concurrency scales under peak network traffic, subtle interleavings of non-deterministic conflict resolutions inevitably produce irrecoverable business state divergence."* Thus, (B) contradicts the text.

- **Evaluating (D):**
  - The passage presents an inherent tradeoff: immediate local acknowledgments without cross-datacenter quorum abandon strong isolation and serializability. The text does not support the claim that protocol-level serializability can be achieved without sacrificing immediate local acknowledgments.

Hence, (C) is the correct option.

An advanced prototyping laboratory uses three automated 3D printing systems, designated as $X, Y,$ and $Z$, to manufacture a batch of aerospace components. Working independently at constant rates:
- System $X$ can fabricate the complete batch in $10\text{ days}$.
- System $Y$ can fabricate the complete batch in $15\text{ days}$.
- System $Z$ can fabricate the complete batch in $30\text{ days}$.

The fabrication process begins with all three systems running concurrently from Day 1. However:
- System $X$ is powered down for maintenance $2\text{ days}$ before the final fabrication is achieved.
- System $Y$ is powered down for maintenance $3\text{ days}$ before the final fabrication is achieved.
- System $Z$ runs uninterrupted until the entire batch is completely finished.

The total duration required to manufacture the complete batch, measured in days from the start, is ______ days.

### Solution
Let us solve this using the LCM unit model and the *"Cancel the holiday / Don't let them leave"* method:

1. **Determine Total Fabrication Work:**
   $$\text{Total Work} = \text{LCM}(10, 15, 30) = 30\text{ units}$$

2. **Compute Daily Rates (Efficiency):**
   - Rate of $X$:
     $$R_X = \frac{30}{10} = 3\text{ units/day}$$
   - Rate of $Y$:
     $$R_Y = \frac{30}{15} = 2\text{ units/day}$$
   - Rate of $Z$:
     $$R_Z = \frac{30}{30} = 1\text{ unit/day}$$
   - Combined rate when all three operate together:
     $$R_{\text{all}} = R_X + R_Y + R_Z = 3 + 2 + 1 = 6\text{ units/day}$$

3. **Apply "Cancel the Holiday / Don't Let Them Leave" Method:**
   - Instead of setting up reverse timeline equations, consider what extra units would have been produced if systems $X$ and $Y$ had not been powered down early:
     - If System $X$ had operated for those last $2\text{ days}$, it would have fabricated:
       $$\text{Extra Work}_X = 2\text{ days} \times 3\text{ units/day} = 6\text{ units}$$
     - If System $Y$ had operated for those last $3\text{ days}$, it would have fabricated:
       $$\text{Extra Work}_Y = 3\text{ days} \times 2\text{ units/day} = 6\text{ units}$$
   - Add these extra units to the initial batch total:
     $$\text{Adjusted Total Work} = 30 + 6 + 6 = 42\text{ units}$$
   - Now, all three systems ($X, Y, Z$) operate concurrently from start to finish:
     $$\text{Total Days} = \frac{\text{Adjusted Total Work}}{R_{\text{all}}} = \frac{42\text{ units}}{6\text{ units/day}} = 7\text{ days}$$

4. **Verification via Stage-by-Stage Working:**
   - Total duration = $7\text{ days}$.
   - **Days 1 to 4 (First 4 days):** All three ($X, Y, Z$) operate:
     $$\text{Work} = 4 \times (3 + 2 + 1) = 4 \times 6 = 24\text{ units}$$
   - **Day 5 (1 day):** $Y$ is powered down (3 days before finish, so after Day 4); $X$ and $Z$ operate:
     $$\text{Work} = 1 \times (3 + 1) = 4\text{ units}$$
     $$\text{Cumulative Work} = 24 + 4 = 28\text{ units}$$
   - **Days 6 and 7 (Last 2 days):** $X$ is also powered down (2 days before finish, so after Day 5); only $Z$ operates:
     $$\text{Work} = 2 \times 1 = 2\text{ units}$$
     $$\text{Total Cumulative Work} = 28 + 2 = 30\text{ units}$$
   - Exactly matches the required $30\text{ units}$ batch.

Hence, the answer is 7.

## Q8
type: MCQ
marks: 2
subject: general-aptitude
answer: A

Two technicians, $X$ and $Y$, working independently at constant rates, can complete the optical fiber wiring of a data server room in $12\text{ days}$ and $18\text{ days}$, respectively.

They agree to work on alternate days to avoid physical interference in the narrow server aisle:
- Technician $X$ works alone on Day 1.
- Technician $Y$ works alone on Day 2.
- Technician $X$ works alone on Day 3, and so on, continuing strictly in this alternating sequence until the wiring is finished.

The total time required to complete the entire wiring work is:

(A) $14\frac{1}{3}\text{ days}$
(B) $14\frac{1}{2}\text{ days}$
(C) $14\frac{2}{3}\text{ days}$
(D) $15\text{ days}$

### Solution
Let us solve using the LCM discrete 2-day cycle model:

1. **Determine Total Work:**
   $$\text{Total Work} = \text{LCM}(12, 18) = 36\text{ units}$$

2. **Calculate Daily Rates:**
   - Rate of Technician $X$:
     $$R_X = \frac{36}{12} = 3\text{ units/day}$$
   - Rate of Technician $Y$:
     $$R_Y = \frac{36}{18} = 2\text{ units/day}$$

3. **Compute Work Done in One Complete 2-Day Cycle:**
   - Day 1 (Technician $X$): $3\text{ units}$
   - Day 2 (Technician $Y$): $2\text{ units}$
   - Work in $1\text{ cycle } (2\text{ days}) = 3 + 2 = 5\text{ units}$.

4. **Determine the Number of Full Cycles:**
   - We divide $36$ by $5$:
     $$36 = 7 \times 5 + 1$$
   - In $7$ full 2-day cycles:
     $$\text{Time} = 7 \times 2 = 14\text{ days}$$
     $$\text{Work Completed} = 7 \times 5 = 35\text{ units}$$
   - Remaining work to be completed:
     $$\text{Remaining Work} = 36 - 35 = 1\text{ unit}$$

5. **Determine Who Works on Day 15 and Time Taken:**
   - The 7th cycle ends with Technician $Y$ on Day 14.
   - Day 15 begins a new cycle, so **Technician $X$** takes the turn.
   - Technician $X$ works at a rate of $3\text{ units/day}$.
   - Time taken by $X$ to finish the remaining $1\text{ unit}$:
     $$\text{Time} = \frac{\text{Remaining Units}}{R_X} = \frac{1\text{ unit}}{3\text{ units/day}} = \frac{1}{3}\text{ day}$$

6. **Total Time:**
   $$\text{Total Time} = 14\text{ days} + \frac{1}{3}\text{ day} = 14\frac{1}{3}\text{ days}$$

Evaluating the options:
- (A) **$14\frac{1}{3}\text{ days}$** is correct.
- (B) $14\frac{1}{2}\text{ days}$ is the classic GATE distractor trap resulting from erroneously assuming Technician $Y$ works on Day 15 ($1/2 = 0.5\text{ days}$).
- (C) $14\frac{2}{3}\text{ days}$ is incorrect.
- (D) $15\text{ days}$ mistakenly rounds up the fractional work to a full day.

Hence, (A) is the correct option.

## Q9

answer: 16

An industrial cooling reservoir is equipped with three pipes: two inlet pipes, $A$ and $B$, and an outlet drain pipe $C$.
- Pipe $A$ alone can fill the empty reservoir in $15\text{ hours}$.
- Pipe $B$ alone can fill the empty reservoir in $20\text{ hours}$.
- Pipe $C$ alone can empty the completely full reservoir in $30\text{ hours}$.

The reservoir is initially empty. All three pipes ($A, B,$ and $C$) are opened simultaneously at 6:00 AM.
- After $4\text{ hours}$ (at 10:00 AM), drain pipe $C$ is closed, while pipes $A$ and $B$ remain open.
- After another $1\text{ hour}$ (at 11:00 AM), pipe $A$ is also closed, while pipe $B$ continues to operate alone until the reservoir is completely filled.

The total time taken from 6:00 AM until the reservoir is completely filled to capacity is ______ hours.

### Solution
Let us solve using the multi-stage unit rate model:

1. **Determine Total Reservoir Capacity:**
   $$\text{Capacity} = \text{LCM}(15, 20, 30) = 60\text{ units}$$

2. **Compute Individual Hourly Rates:**
   - Rate of Inlet $A$:
     $$R_A = \frac{+60}{15} = +4\text{ units/hour}$$
   - Rate of Inlet $B$:
     $$R_B = \frac{+60}{20} = +3\text{ units/hour}$$
   - Rate of Drain $C$:
     $$R_C = \frac{-60}{30} = -2\text{ units/hour}$$

3. **Stage-by-Stage Breakdown:**

   - **Stage 1 (6:00 AM to 10:00 AM — Duration = 4 hours):**
     - Pipes $A, B,$ and $C$ are all open.
     - Combined Rate:
       $$R_{\text{stage 1}} = R_A + R_B - R_C = 4 + 3 - 2 = +5\text{ units/hour}$$
     - Work Done in 4 hours:
       $$\text{Work}_1 = 4\text{ hours} \times 5\text{ units/hour} = 20\text{ units}$$
     - Remaining Capacity:
       $$\text{Remaining}_1 = 60 - 20 = 40\text{ units}$$

   - **Stage 2 (10:00 AM to 11:00 AM — Duration = 1 hour):**
     - Drain pipe $C$ is closed; pipes $A$ and $B$ are open.
     - Combined Rate:
       $$R_{\text{stage 2}} = R_A + R_B = 4 + 3 = +7\text{ units/hour}$$
     - Work Done in 1 hour:
       $$\text{Work}_2 = 1\text{ hour} \times 7\text{ units/hour} = 7\text{ units}$$
     - Remaining Capacity:
       $$\text{Remaining}_2 = 40 - 7 = 33\text{ units}$$

   - **Stage 3 (From 11:00 AM onwards until full):**
     - Pipe $A$ is closed; only Pipe $B$ runs.
     - Rate:
       $$R_{\text{stage 3}} = R_B = +3\text{ units/hour}$$
     - Time required for Pipe $B$ to fill the remaining $33\text{ units}$:
       $$T_3 = \frac{33\text{ units}}{3\text{ units/hour}} = 11\text{ hours}$$

4. **Calculate Total Duration:**
   $$\text{Total Time} = T_1 + T_2 + T_3 = 4\text{ hours} + 1\text{ hour} + 11\text{ hours} = 16\text{ hours}$$

Hence, the answer is 16.

## Q10
type: MSQ
marks: 2
subject: general-aptitude
answer: A, B

Which of the following sentences is/are grammatically CORRECT?

(A) The software architect recommended refactoring the monolithic codebase, standardizing the API specifications, and automating the integration test suite.
(B) If the network latency were to exceed the critical threshold, the load balancer would automatically redirect traffic to the secondary cluster.
(C) Having completed the performance profiling, the memory leak was immediately eliminated by the engineering team.
(D) Neither the hardware sensors nor the telemetry controller were damaged during the high-voltage surge test.

### Solution
Let us evaluate each sentence against the core grammatical rules:

- **Statement (A):**
  - Examines **Parallelism in Series (Rule 2)**.
  - The verb *"recommended"* governs three coordinated objects connected by commas and *"and"*:
    1. *refactoring the monolithic codebase* (gerund phrase)
    2. *standardizing the API specifications* (gerund phrase)
    3. *automating the integration test suite* (gerund phrase)
  - All three elements maintain exact grammatical symmetry using gerund forms.
  - Thus, statement (A) is **grammatically correct**.

- **Statement (B):**
  - Examines **Conditionals & Subjunctive Mood (Rule 3)**.
  - The sentence describes a hypothetical or future conditional condition using the subjunctive: *"If [subject] were to [verb] ... [subject] would [verb]"*.
  - Even though *"the network latency"* is singular, the formal subjunctive mood correctly employs **were** (not *"was"*), paired with the conditional auxiliary **would redirect** in the main clause.
  - Thus, statement (B) is **grammatically correct**.

- **Statement (C):**
  - Examines **Dangling Modifiers (Rule 4)**.
  - The sentence opens with an introductory participial clause: *"Having completed the performance profiling..."*
  - In English grammar, an introductory modifier must be immediately followed by the noun that actually performed the action. Here, the noun immediately following the comma is *"the memory leak"*.
  - The memory leak did not perform the performance profiling; the engineering team did.
  - To be grammatically correct, it must read: *"Having completed the performance profiling, the engineering team immediately eliminated the memory leak."*
  - Thus, statement (C) contains a dangling modifier and is **grammatically incorrect**.

- **Statement (D):**
  - Examines **Subject-Verb Agreement and the Proximity Rule (Rule 1)**.
  - When two subjects are joined by the correlative conjunction *"Neither ... nor ..."*, the verb must agree in grammatical number with the **closer** subject.
  - Here, the subjects are *"the hardware sensors"* (plural) and *"the telemetry controller"* (singular).
  - The closer subject to the verb is *"the telemetry controller"*, which is singular.
  - Therefore, the verb must be the singular **was damaged**, not the plural *"were damaged"*.
  - Thus, statement (D) violates the proximity rule and is **grammatically incorrect**.

Therefore, only statements (A) and (B) are grammatically correct.

Hence, the correct choices are (A) and (B).

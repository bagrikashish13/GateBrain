# Root Set R1 · Good vs. Bad Roots & The 2-Step Filter

## TL;DR
Verbal questions test polarity ($+$ vs. $-$) and subject context. Decode words in 2 steps: first check prefix tone, then check the base/suffix to identify *who or what* it applies to.

## Core idea
- **Polarity map:**
  - **Positive ($+$):** `bene` / `bon` (good, well), `eu` (pleasant, sweet, harmonious).
  - **Negative ($-$):** `mal` (bad, evil, ill), `caco` (harsh, discordant), `dys` (abnormal, painful, bad), `mis` (wrong, false, hatred).
- **The 2-step filter method:**
  1. *Step 1 (Polarity):* Match sentence tone (+ vs -) to eliminate 2 wrong options.
  2. *Step 2 (Subject / Base Clue):* Look at the second root/suffix to see if it describes a person's will, a physical condition, polite speech, or a factual label.

## Where I got confused → what made it click
- **Confusing words with identical polarity (e.g. *benevolent* vs. *benign*):**
  - `-volent` (*volo* = to wish/will): describes conscious human intention/kindness (*benevolent leader*).
  - `-nign` (*genus* = physical nature): describes inherent physical or medical harmlessness (*benign growth*).
- **Confusing *euphemism* vs. *misnomer* vs. *euphony*:**
  - `-phem` (*pheme* = voice/speech): intentionally substituting a mild/polite word for a harsh one (*"taking early retirement"* instead of *"dismissed"*, *"pre-owned"* instead of *"used"*).
  - `-phon` (*phone* = sound): a pleasant acoustic sound (*euphony*), opposite of jarring noise (*cacophony*). Do not confuse pleasant speech with pleasant sound!
  - `-nomer` (*nomen* = name): a factually incorrect label (*lead pencil* is a misnomer because it's graphite).

## GATE traps
- **Using personal-will adjectives on inanimate or medical conditions:** A tumor is never *benevolent* or *malevolent*; it is *benign* or *malignant*.
- **Over-memorizing dictionaries:** GATE CSE has only 1–2 direct vocabulary questions (1–2 marks). Only ~70 core roots across 14 sets are needed.

## Example
> **Question:** In corporate announcements, calling layoffs *"headcount rationalization"* is a classic ________.  
> (A) misnomer &nbsp;&nbsp; (B) euphemism &nbsp;&nbsp; (C) cacophony &nbsp;&nbsp; (D) malevolence  
> **Solution:** (B). It is polite/softened phrasing to avoid blunt impact (`eu-` + `phem`), not an accidental naming error.

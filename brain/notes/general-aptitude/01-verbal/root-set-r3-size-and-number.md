# Root Set R3 · Size, Scope & Number

## TL;DR
Roots of size and number establish the scale or multiplicity of an entity. Look for `pan`/`omni` for universality ("all"), `magn`/`mega` for greatness/scale, and `mono`/`bi`/`poly` for count.

## Core idea
- **Universal / All-encompassing:**
  - `omni-` (Latin: all): *omnipresent* (everywhere), *omniscient* (all-knowing), *omnipotent* (all-powerful).
  - `pan-` (Greek: all/whole): *panacea* (cure-all), *panorama* (all-round view), *panoply* (full impressive array).
- **Scale & Magnitude:**
  - `magn-` / `maj-`: great, large (*magnanimous* = noble/great-spirited, *magnitude* = size/importance).
  - `mega-`: huge, grand (*megalomania* = obsession with greatness/power).
  - `micro-`: small, fine (*microcosm* = a small model representing a larger whole).
- **Number & Multiplicity:**
  - `mono-`: one (*monolithic* = rigid/single uniform mass, *monotonous* = lacking variation).
  - `bi-` / `du-`: two, double (*bipartite* = two-part, *duplicity* = double-dealing, deceit).
  - `tri-`: three (*trichotomy* = division into three parts).
  - `poly-`: many (*polyglot* = multilingual, *polymath* = expert in many subjects, *polymorphism* = multiple forms).

## Where I got confused → what made it click
- **Panacea vs. Panoply:** Both start with `pan-` (all), but *panacea* has `-acea` (*cure* $\implies$ universal remedy), while *panoply* refers to a complete suit of armor or a complete, dazzling display.
- **Duplicity is character deceit, not just "doubleness":** `duplicity` implies two-faced behavior (deceit), whereas `duplicate` is a neutral copy.
- **Magnanimous vs. Megalomania:** `magnanimous` is morally great and forgiving (*animus* = soul/mind), while `megalomania` is an unhealthy delusion of self-importance.

## GATE traps
- **Confusing scientific prefixes with moral traits:** In sentences about ethical conduct or negotiations, words like *duplicity* or *magnanimity* are common answers.
- **Distractors sharing identical prefixes:** GATE often pairs two words with `pan-` (e.g., *panacea* vs. *panoply*) to test whether you know the base root, not just the base prefix.

## Example
> **Question:** The proposed algorithm was marketed as a complete ________ for all synchronization hazards, but in practice, it introduced severe priority inversion.  
> (A) panoply &nbsp;&nbsp; (B) microcosm &nbsp;&nbsp; (C) panacea &nbsp;&nbsp; (D) magnitude  
> **Solution:** (C). *Panacea* (`pan-` all + `akos` cure) means a universal remedy.

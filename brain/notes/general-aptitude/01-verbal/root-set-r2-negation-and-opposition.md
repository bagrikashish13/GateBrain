# Root Set R2 · Negation & Opposition

## TL;DR
Prefixes of negation (`a/an`, `in/im/il/ir`, `un/non/dis`, `anti/contra`) reverse meaning. Crucial rule: a negative prefix on a negative root forms a positive shield ($-\times- = +$). In MCQs, "opposite twins" ($X$ vs. $\text{not-}X$) usually contain the answer.

## Core idea
- **The "Without / Lacking" prefix:**
  - `a-` / `an-`: without or lacking.
    - *Apathy* (`a-` without + `pathos` feeling) = lack of feeling, concern, or enthusiasm.
    - *Anarchy* (`an-` without + `arch` ruler) = absence of government or authoritative control.
    - *Anomaly* (`an-` without + `homalos` regular) = deviation from standard, regular order.
    - *Anonymous* (`an-` without + `onym` name) = nameless.
- **The "Not / Cannot" cluster:**
  - `in-` assimilates before consonants: `im-` (before p/m), `il-` (before l), `ir-` (before r).
    - *Infallible* (`in-` not + `fall` err) = incapable of making mistakes or errors.
    - *Immutable* (`im-` not + `mut` change) = unchangeable over time.
    - *Illicit* (`il-` not + `licit` allowed) = forbidden by rules or laws.
    - *Irrevocable* (`ir-` not + `voc` call) = unalterable, cannot be recalled.
- **The "Against / Opposite" cluster:**
  - `anti-`: against, hostile to.
    - *Antipathy* (`anti-` against + `pathos` feeling) = deep-seated aversion or hostility.
    - *Antithesis* (`anti-` against + `thesis` proposition) = the direct or exact opposite.
  - `contra-`: contrary, in opposition.
    - *Contradict* (`contra-` against + `dict` speak) = assert the opposite, speak against.
    - *Contravention* (`contra-` against + `venire` come) = action violating a law or condition.

## Where I got confused → what made it click
- **Fear of infinite vocabulary:** GATE CSE Verbal is only 4–5 marks total, with only 1 pure vocabulary question. GATE tests standard academic/technical terms, never obscure dictionary trivia. A closed set of 14 root sets (~70 roots) covers what's needed.
- **The Double-Negative Root rule:** When a negative prefix (`in-`, `un-`) attaches to a negative/flaw root (`vulnerable`, `fallible`), it produces a **positive (+)** shield meaning:
  - *Vulnerable* (prone to attack/harm) $\to$ *Invulnerable* (shielded, cannot be harmed).
  - *Fallible* (prone to error) $\to$ *Infallible* (incapable of error, reliable).
- **The "Opposite Twins" MCQ heuristic:** When two options are mirror opposites ($X$ vs. $\text{not-}X$, like *vulnerable* vs. *invulnerable*), examiners almost always chose them as the target vs. primary distractor. Identify sentence polarity ($+$ or $-$) to pick the correct twin.
- **Amoral vs. Immoral:**
  - *Immoral*: actively violating moral principles (bad/evil).
  - *Amoral*: having no awareness or concept of morality (e.g. an algorithm, machine, or infant).
- **Dispassionate is positive/objective:** Does not mean uncaring or cold; it means unbiased, calm, impartial, and free from emotional prejudice.

## GATE traps
- **Assuming all `in-`/`un-` words are bad:** Words like *invulnerable*, *infallible*, and *unbiased* represent ideal, robust engineering states.
- **Missing context clues:** Check for contrast connectors (*although, despite, whereas*) to predict whether the missing word must be positive (+) or negative (-) before reading options.

## Example
> **Question:** Despite prolonged cyber attacks, the core transaction ledger proved ________, preserving data integrity without any corruption.  
> (A) volatile &nbsp;&nbsp; (B) vulnerable &nbsp;&nbsp; (C) invulnerable &nbsp;&nbsp; (D) illicit  
> **Solution:** (C). The context requires a positive attribute denoting resilience against attacks. *Invulnerable* (`in-` not + *vulnerare* to wound $\implies$ cannot be harmed) fits perfectly. (B) is the opposite twin.

# Root Words and Confusable Pairs

## TL;DR
GATE vocabulary does not test obscure dictionary words. It tests atomic roots that decode dozens of words at once, and a finite set of ~30 confusable pairs (words that sound or look similar). 

## Core idea
- **The Top 10 Atomic Roots for GATE:**
  | Root | Meaning | Examples |
  |---|---|---|
  | `bene / bon` | Good, well | **Benevolent** (wishing good), **Benign** (harmless), **Benefactor** |
  | `mal / male` | Bad, ill | **Malevolent** (spiteful), **Malign** (speak ill of), **Malice** |
  | `chron` | Time | **Anachronism** (out of time), **Chronology**, **Synchronous** |
  | `cred` | Believe, trust | **Credible** (believable), **Credulous** (gullible), **Incredulous** (skeptical) |
  | `loq / loc` | Speak, talk | **Loquacious** (talkative), **Eloquent** (fluent speech), **Colloquial** |
  | `omni` | All | **Omniscient** (all-knowing), **Omnipotent** (all-powerful) |
  | `pauci` | Few, little | **Paucity** (scarcity, shortage) |
  | `luc / lum` | Light, clear | **Lucid** (clear, easy to understand), **Elucidate** (make clear) |
  | `path` | Feeling, disease | **Apathy** (no feeling), **Empathy** (feeling another's pain), **Antipathy** (hatred) |
  | `greg` | Group, herd | **Gregarious** (sociable), **Egregious** (standing out badly), **Congregate** |

- **The High-Yield Confusable Pairs (GATE Favorites):**
  - **Complement vs Compliment:**
    - *Complement (with E):* Completes something or goes well together ("Her skills *complement* the team").
    - *Compliment (with I):* An expression of praise ("He received a *compliment* on his rank").
  - **Stationary vs Stationery:**
    - *Stationary (with A):* Not moving, standing still ("The car remained *stationary*").
    - *Stationery (with E):* Pens, paper, office supplies (Remember: **E** for **E**nvelope / **E**raser!).
  - **Principal vs Principle:**
    - *Principal:* Head of an institute; or main/primary sum ("The *principal* objective", "The *principal* amount").
    - *Principle:* A rule, fundamental truth, or moral value ("A man of *principles*").
  - **Affect vs Effect:**
    - *Affect (Verb):* To influence or alter ("The delay will *affect* the schedule").
    - *Effect (Noun):* The result or consequence ("The *effect* of the decision was positive").
  - **Discrete vs Discreet:**
    - *Discrete (separate 'e's):* Individually separate or distinct ("*Discrete* mathematics").
    - *Discreet:* Careful, prudent, keeping a secret ("Please be *discreet* about this matter").
  - **Eminent vs Imminent:**
    - *Eminent:* Famous, respected, distinguished ("An *eminent* scientist").
    - *Imminent:* About to happen very soon ("The storm is *imminent*").
  - **Elicit vs Illicit:**
    - *Elicit:* To draw out or evoke a reaction ("His speech *elicited* applause").
    - *Illicit:* Illegal or forbidden by law ("*Illicit* trade").

## Where I got confused → what made it click
- **Complement vs Compliment:**
  - *The click:* Compl**e**ment compl**e**tes! Both have an **e**.
- **Stationery vs Stationary:**
  - *The click:* Station**e**ry has an **e** for **e**nvelope, p**e**n, and pap**e**r!
- **Credible vs Credulous:**
  - *The click:* A *story* is cred**ible** (believable). A *person* who believes too easily is cred**ulous** (gullible).

## GATE traps
- **"Affect" as a noun / "Effect" as a verb:** In 99% of GATE questions, *Affect = Action (Verb)* and *Effect = End result (Noun)*.
- **Context Signposts:** Even if options look unfamiliar, transition words reveal the required tone:
  - *Although / Despite / Yet* $\implies$ Opposite sign (+ becomes −).
  - *Moreover / Furthermore / Therefore* $\implies$ Same sign (+ stays +).

## Example
- **GATE 2022:** "The government tried to _______ the fears of the public by providing clear guidelines."
  - Options often test *assuage / mitigate* (make less severe) vs negative sounding words.
- **GATE 2020:** "The speaker gave an _______ presentation that left everyone inspired."
  - Root `loq` / `luc` $\implies$ *eloquent* (fluent) or *lucid* (clear).

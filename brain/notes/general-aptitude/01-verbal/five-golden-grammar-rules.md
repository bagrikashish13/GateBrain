# Five Golden Grammar Rules

## TL;DR
GATE grammar tests only 5 recurring structures: proximity in "either/neither", ignoring modifier phrases between subject and verb, parallel list forms, conditional subjunctive "were/had", and dangling modifiers. Master these five and intuition becomes certainty.

## Core idea
- **Rule 1: Subject-Verb Agreement (Proximity & Intervening Phrases):**
  - **Proximity Rule:** For *"Neither... nor"* and *"Either... or"*, the verb agrees with the **closer** subject.
    - *Example:* "Neither the professor nor the **students were** present."
    - *Example:* "Neither the students nor the **professor was** present."
  - **Intervening Phrase Rule:** Phrases like *"along with"*, *"as well as"*, *"in addition to"*, *"accompanied by"* are NOT conjunctions; they do not change the subject number.
    - *Example:* "The **leader**, along with his followers, **was** arrested." (Subject is *leader*, singular).

- **Rule 2: Parallelism in Series:**
  - Items in a list, comparison, or connected by *"and / but / or"* must share the exact same grammatical shape.
  - *Wrong:* "She likes swimming, to jog, and dance."
  - *Right:* "She likes **swimming, jogging, and dancing**." (All gerunds).
  - *Right:* "She likes **to swim, to jog, and to dance**." (All infinitives).

- **Rule 3: Conditionals (The Subjunctive & "If" Clauses):**
  - **Unreal / Hypothetical (Present/Future):** *If + were / past simple $\to$ would + V1*.
    - *Right:* "If I **were** (not was) you, I **would** accept the offer."
  - **Past Unfulfilled Condition:** *If + had + V3 $\to$ would have + V3*.
    - *Right:* "If he **had studied** harder, he **would have cleared** the cutoff." (Never write *"If he would have studied"*).

- **Rule 4: Dangling Modifiers (Who is doing the action?):**
  - An introductory participle clause (starting with *-ing* or *-ed*) MUST be followed immediately by the noun that actually performs that action.
  - *Wrong:* "Walking into the room, the fan fell on John." (Was the fan walking?).
  - *Right:* "Walking into the room, **John** saw the fan fall."

- **Rule 5: Past Perfect vs Simple Past (Chronology):**
  - Use Past Perfect (*had + V3*) **only** to distinguish the earlier of two past events. Never use it in isolation when simple past suffices.
  - *Right:* "The train **had left** (action 1) before we **reached** (action 2) the station."

## Where I got confused → what made it click
- **"As well as" vs "And":**
  - *Confusion:* Treating *"A as well as B"* like *"A and B"* and making the verb plural.
  - *The click:* Put brackets around `(as well as B)`. Read only the subject before the bracket: *"The captain (as well as the players) is ready."*
- **Hypothetical conditionals:**
  - *Confusion:* Saying *"If I was rich"*.
  - *The click:* In hypothetical or impossible wishes, English always uses **were** for all persons (I were, he were, she were).

## GATE traps
- **"One of the [Plural Noun] who [Plural Verb]":**
  - *"He is one of the students who **were** selected."* (The antecedent of *who* is *students*, so plural!).
  - *"He is the **only one** of the students who **was** selected."* (*Only one* makes it singular!).
- **Each / Every / Neither / Either as subject:** Always takes a singular verb.
  - *"Each of the candidates **has** submitted the form."*

## Example
- **GATE 2021:** "Neither the director nor the actors _______ present at the venue."
  - Subject closer to blank = *actors* (plural) $\implies$ **were**.
- **GATE 2019:** "If the committee had reviewed the proposal earlier, the project _______ delayed."
  - Condition is *had reviewed* $\implies$ result must be **would not have been**.

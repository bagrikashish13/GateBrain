# Conditional sentences

## TL;DR
Inverted conditionals omit "if": **"Had he known..."** is strictly identical to **"If he had known..."** (Third Conditional) and requires **"would have + V3"** in the main clause.

## Core idea
- **Conditional types summary:**
  | Type | If-clause | Main clause | Usage |
  | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
  | **Zero** | Simple present (`if it rains`) | Simple present (`grass gets wet`) | Universal truth |
  | **1st** | Simple present (`if it rains`) | `will + V1` (`I will stay inside`) | Real / likely future |
  | **2nd** | Simple past (`if I knew`) | `would + V1` (`I would tell you`) | Unreal present/future |
  | **3rd** | Past perfect (`if I had known`) | `would have + V3` (`I would have told`) | Unreal past |

- **Inversion rules (omitting "if"):**
  - **1st conditional:** `Should you need help, please call me.` ($= \text{If you should need}$)
  - **2nd conditional:** `Were I in your position, I would accept.` ($= \text{If I were}$)
  - **3rd conditional:** `Had they warned us, we would have prepared.` ($= \text{If they had warned us}$)

## Where I got confused → what made it click
- **"Had" triggering another past perfect:** Thinking "had he known" must match with "he had not disclosed" $\to$ "Had" here is purely the inverted conditional marker for the *if-clause*; the main consequence clause always requires modal + perfect infinitive (`would have + V3`).

## GATE traps
- **Using simple future with past condition:** Pairing "Had he known" with "will not disclose" (mixing past unreal premise with real future consequence).
- **Double past perfect:** Pairing "Had he known" with "had not disclosed" (main clause needs a modal auxiliary like *would*).

## Example
**Problem (GATE CSE):**
"He did not know that the information was confidential; had he known, he ______ it to the press."

- **If-clause (inverted):** `had he known` $\equiv$ `if he had known` (3rd conditional).
- **Required main clause:** `would/could/might + have + past participle` $\implies$ **would not have disclosed**.
